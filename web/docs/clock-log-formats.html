Clock-log formats

src/clock_log.rs reads a measured clock log into one normalised phase series: a uniform grid of (time_s, phase_s) samples, with the sample interval tau0 stated or found, every gap counted and the log's units named. Eight formats are read, each a documented subset. Anything outside the subset is refused with an error that names what was found, rather than read approximately.

Abbreviations. RINEX is the Receiver Independent Exchange Format. TICC is the TAPR (Tucson Amateur Packet Radio) time-interval counter. ADEV is the overlapping Allan deviation, TDEV the time deviation and MTIE the maximum time interval error. tau0 is the sample interval in seconds.

Where a log can be used#

  • telecom-timing: [series.clock_log] in place of series.samples or series.csv_path. The log's phase, in ns, is the time-error series checked against the masks, and the report gains a clock_log block saying how it was read.
  • The clock kind: a [clock_log] table adds a measured block to the run: how the log was read, the SHA-256 of the table (input_sha256; the run's scenario_hash does not cover the table) and of the phase samples, and the overlapping ADEV, TDEV and MTIE at octave averaging factors. It needs runs = 1. The simulated holdover is unchanged.

Without the table, both kinds parse, hash and report exactly as before.

[clock_log]                 # or [series.clock_log] in a telecom-timing scenario
format = "ticc"             # "rinex-clk", "timelab", "ticc", "ptp4l", "phc2sys", "ts2phc",
                            # "chrony-tracking" or "chrony-statistics"
path = "logs/ticc.txt"      # or text = "..." inline (the only choice in WebAssembly)
channel = "chA"             # TICC: needed when the log holds more than one channel
gaps = "linear"             # "refuse" (default), "longest" or "linear"
# clock = "G08"             # RINEX, linuxptp, chrony: needed when the log holds more than one clock or source
# tau0_s = 1.0              # overrides a header and the detected spacing
# units = "ns"              # TimeLab phase units: s (default), ms, us, ns, ps
# data = "phase"            # TimeLab: phase (default) or frequency

The formats#

rinex-clk: RINEX clock files, versions 2 and 3.00 to 3.04 (including the 3.04 nine-character name field). The header is read only for its TIME SYSTEM ID and is skipped up to END OF HEADER. Of the data records, only the AS (satellite) and AR (receiver or station) records of one clock, selected by name, are kept. Their first value field, the clock bias in seconds, is the phase. Continuation lines and the CR, DR and MS records are skipped. Epochs are the record's calendar fields, as seconds from 1980-01-06 00:00:00 in the file's own time system, with no conversion.

timelab: TimeLab-style ASCII phase or frequency logs, the plain-text form that TimeLab exports and its ASCII import and Stable32 read. There is one reading per line: either a single column at a uniform tau0, or two columns time value. Columns may be separated by white space, commas or semicolons.

  • Lines starting with #, ; or % are comments. A comment of the form key = value (or key: value) sets tau0 (s), units (s, ms, us, ns or ps, for phase), data (phase or frequency) or time_units (s or mjd, for the first of two columns).
  • One leading non-numeric line is taken as a column header.
  • Frequency data (fractional frequency) is accepted as a single column only. It is integrated to phase as x[i+1] = x[i] + y[i]·tau0 (NIST SP 1065, 2008, §4.1).
  • TimeLab's binary and .tim project files are not read.

ticc: logs of the TAPR TICC counter (TICC Operation Manual, revision of 23 November 2016, "measurement modes"). Every data field is seconds with 12 decimal places.

  • Timestamp-mode lines (<seconds> chA, <seconds> chB) and the TimeLab mode's pseudo-timestamp channel (chC) are event time stamps. The phase of the selected channel is its time stamp minus the nominal tick: x[k] = t[k] − t[0] − n[k]·tau0, where n[k] is the whole number of tau0 since the first event. A missed event is a gap.
  • Interval-mode lines (<seconds> TI(A->B), or any label starting TI) are read directly as phase, one reading per tau0. The scenario must state tau0_s, because the line carries no time.
  • Period-mode and Debug-mode output is refused. A channel whose readings do not increase is reported as Period mode.
  • Lines starting with #, and lines that are not <number> <label> (such as the start-up menu), are skipped and counted.

linuxptp and chrony logs#

These five formats read what a running time daemon writes about its own clock. They are for checking a timing chain after the fact (a boundary clock, a grandmaster's PHC, a chrony client) with the same estimators as the other logs. They do not read a daemon's configuration, and nothing here talks to a daemon.

ptp4l, phc2sys, ts2phc: the servo lines of linuxptp, each format reading only lines of its own program. A reading is a line of the form

ptp4l[5003.000]: master offset         40 s2 freq   +1480 path delay       497
phc2sys[7000.000]: CLOCK_REALTIME phc offset       -30 s2 freq    +100 delay   1200
ts2phc[8000.000]: /dev/ptp0 offset          3 s2 freq         -2
  • The time stamp is the first [seconds.fraction] in the line (the daemon's monotonic clock). It is also read after a syslog prefix, where a process id such as ptp4l[812]: has no fraction and is not taken.
  • The phase is the logged offset, in nanoseconds, converted to seconds. It is the daemon's measured time error of its slave clock against its reference, as logged: positive when the slave is ahead. It is the error left under servo control, not a free-running clock's drift.
  • The words between the time stamp and offset name the source (master, eth0 master for a [eth0] prefix, CLOCK_REALTIME phc, /dev/ptp0). A log with more than one source needs clock = "<that text>"; the error lists them.
  • Readings before the servo first reports s2 (locked) are left out and counted in n_skipped_lines: s0 and s1 are the unlocked and step states, whose offsets are pull-in transients. A servo that never reaches s2 is refused. A line with no state field is read as it is.
  • Summary lines (rms ... max ...), delay and selected best master messages, and lines of other programs are skipped and counted.
  • Time stamps that go backwards (a restarted daemon) are refused; split the log.

chrony-tracking, chrony-statistics: chrony's log tracking and log statistics files, read by column position. A reading is a line with at least 14 columns (tracking) or 13 (statistics) that starts with a YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS UTC date and time. The column header line is skipped and counted; the ==== rule under it is skipped without being counted.

  • Tracking: column 7, the local offset on the clock update, in seconds. The IP address or reference id changes with the selected source, so without clock every line is read as one local clock. clock = "<id>" keeps one reference id.
  • Statistics: column 5, the estimated offset of a source, in seconds. There is one series per source address, and the log needs clock = "<address>" when it holds more than one.
  • Epochs are seconds from 1980-01-06 00:00:00 UTC, with no leap-second handling. Chrony writes a line per update or per source sample, so the spacing is not uniform. tau0 is the median spacing; a reading under half a tau0 after the one kept before it is dropped and counted in n_duplicates, and a longer spacing is a gap, which gaps = "refuse" reports. Give tau0_s and gaps = "linear" for a log with a steady poll interval.

The chrony columns and the linuxptp line layouts are those of the daemons' public documentation. The fixtures in tests/fixtures/clock_log/linuxptp_chrony/ are written for Kshana with made-up values (see its NOTICE.md); no real log was read, so a layout that differs between daemon versions can still be refused or misread. A line that does not match is skipped and counted, never guessed.

tau0 and gaps#

When the log carries time stamps and no tau0 is given, tau0 is the median spacing of consecutive time stamps, rounded to four significant figures, and the source is reported as median-spacing. A counter's spacing differs from nominal by the reference's frequency offset, parts in 10^6 or less. A tau0_s in the scenario always wins (source input), then a tau0 header comment (source header).

Samples are placed on the grid t0 + i·tau0, and a missing epoch is a gap. Readings more than 1 % of tau0 off the grid are dropped and counted, as are duplicates (the first is kept). The gap policy is one of:

  • refuse (the default): any gap is an error that counts the gaps;
  • longest: keep the longest gap-free run;
  • linear: fill interior gaps by linear interpolation, and report the number filled.

The estimators need a gap-free record, which is why gaps are resolved here.

Evidence#

MODELLED. This is a parser, and its output feeds estimators that are validated elsewhere against allantools. The unit tests in src/clock_log.rs use small hand-written logs, and for the linuxptp and chrony formats the fixtures in tests/fixtures/clock_log/linuxptp_chrony/. tests/clock_log_allantools.rs runs the comparison pre-registered in tests/fixtures/clock_log/PREREGISTRATION.md: three seeded synthetic logs, one per format, read back by an independent reader written from this page by the same author, with the ADEV, TDEV and MTIE checked against allantools 2024.6; that comparison covers the first three formats only, and the linuxptp and chrony readers have parser tests but no oracle comparison. The logs are synthetic, so nothing here describes a real clock or counter.
