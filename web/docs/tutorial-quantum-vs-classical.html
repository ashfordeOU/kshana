Tutorial 3 — Quantum vs classical GNSS resilience (capstone)

Kinds: spoof + hybrid · Scenarios: scenarios/spoof-attack.toml, scenarios/hybrid-pnt.toml (teaching copies: scenarios/security.toml, scenarios/hybrid.toml) · Difficulty: intermediate · ~35 min

The capstone. You will run a spoofing-detector scenario and a full fused positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) suite, and learn to read security, integrity, and dead-reckoning together — the whole thesis: quantum inertial + optical timing buy resilience that GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System) denial would otherwise take away.

Part A — Security: catching a time spoof#

An attacker injects a slowly-ramping false GNSS time (0.1 ns/s, starting at t = 60 s). The receiver cross-checks the asserted time against its own clock’s coasted prediction and flags the spoof when the discrepancy passes the detection bound. The detector is a two-sided χ²₁ / Neyman–Pearson energy test at a false-alarm probability P_fa = 0.01; the Security figure of merit is 1 − P_md (one minus the probability of missed detection) at the spec-sized (20 ns) spoof magnitude.

cargo run -- scenarios/spoof-attack.toml

Summary:

scenario 2b6bd22c3b80 | spoof LinearRamp { rate_ns_per_s: 0.1 } vs 20.000 ns spec (P_fa 0.010) | quantum security 1.000 (P_md 0.000, MC 0.000) detected 70s | classical security 0.558 (P_md 0.442, MC 0.444) detected 430s
  • Quantum: security 1.000, detected at 70 s. The optical clock’s detection floor is a fraction of a nanosecond, so it flags the spoof almost immediately — far below the 20 ns spec.
  • Classical: security 0.558, detected at 430 s. The chip-scale atomic clock’s (CSAC’s) own coast noise over the window is comparable to 20 ns, so it often can’t tell the spoof from its own drift — it misses 44 % of the time.

The non-circular oracle: analytic vs Monte-Carlo P_md#

The detector’s missed-detection probability is computed two independent ways that must agree: a closed-form χ²₁ tail (the Neyman–Pearson result) and a Monte-Carlo (MC) estimate. For the CSAC the analytic P_md is 0.442 and the MC P_md is 0.444 — they agree to 0.002, well inside the ~few × 1/√N sampling error. For the optical clock both are 0.000. Two separate computations of the same probability agreeing is the non-circular cross-check (Kay, Fundamentals of Statistical Signal Processing: Detection Theory; standard NP / χ² detection). tests/tutorials.rs::tutorial3_spoof_analytic_matches_montecarlo asserts |analytic_pmd − mc_pmd| < 0.05 for both clocks and quantum security_fom > 0.9.

Part B — Hybrid PNT: the full fused suite#

Now the capstone scenario fuses an optical clock + a cold-atom inertial measurement unit (IMU) (quantum suite) against a CSAC + a navigation-grade IMU (classical suite), through the same 6600 s (1.83 h) GNSS outage, against a 20 ns timing spec and a 100 m position spec. Both suites re-anchor their clocks every 60 s over the same optical inter-satellite time-transfer link, modelled with a 1 ps per-resync jitter ([resync] in the scenario; src/hybrid.rs builds one optical-isl link for either suite).

cargo run -- scenarios/hybrid-pnt.toml

Summary:

scenario f33d734ecc51 | quantum PNT-holdover 6600s (t 6600s/p 6600s) integrity 0.998 security n/a (no attack) | classical PNT-holdover 350s (t 6600s/p 350s) integrity 1.000 security n/a (no attack)

Read the (t …/p …) split — timing holdover vs position holdover:

  • Quantum: PNT-holdover 6600 s — holds both timing (t 6600 s) and position (p 6600 s) for the whole outage.
  • Classical: PNT-holdover 350 s — timing holds the full 6600 s (t 6600 s), because the optical time-transfer resync keeps even the classical clock locked. The suite is position-limited at p 350 s: the nav-grade IMU is the weak link. This is the fusion thesis — isolate the classical suite’s failure to its inertial sensor.

integrity 0.998 for the quantum suite is real and is not a bug. It is the filter’s self-consistency over noisy resync — not an aviation integrity figure (horizontal or vertical protection level, HPL/VPL). Don’t over-read the word “integrity” here; see docs/INTEGRITY.md.

The non-circular oracle: the 350 s position-holdover#

The 350 s figure is dead-reckoning physics, not a fitted number. A constant accelerometer bias b drives position error as ½·b·T². Crossing the 100 m spec:

Navigation-grade (classical), b = 1.57e-3 m/s^2:
  T = sqrt(2 * 100 / 1.57e-3) = 357 s

Cold-atom (quantum), b = 5.88e-7 m/s^2 (Templier et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.13209):
  T = sqrt(2 * 100 / 5.88e-7) = 18,443 s >> 6600 s outage -> holds the full outage

The engine reports 350 s, the last 10 s grid point before its crossing and 2 % short of the bias-only 357 s: the scenario’s accelerometers also carry velocity random-walk noise (q_va), which adds to the bias drift, so the modelled error reaches 100 m a little sooner.

Authoritative law: Groves, Principles of GNSS, Inertial, and Multisensor Integrated Navigation (2nd ed.), dead-reckoning error growth. These two ½bT² values account for the engine’s 350 s / 6600 s split: a closed-form physics check, external to the filter.

You can see the inertial weak link directly in the dead-reckoning scenario (scenarios/inertial.toml, kind inertial): quantum holdover 6600s p95 41.39m | classical holdover 350s p95 30629.9m: the navigation-grade sensor diverges to tens of kilometres.

How to read the three figures together#

Figure What it means here What it does not mean
Security analytic spoof-detectability bound (1 − P_md) for a configured attack; the hybrid summary prints n/a (no attack) because that scenario configures none not a multi-satellite RAIM (receiver autonomous integrity monitoring) detector
Integrity filter self-consistency (samples inside the k-σ bound) not aviation HPL/VPL/RAIM integrity
PNT-holdover time in spec after GNSS loss, split into timing and position not a 2-D CEP (circular error probable) or 2DRMS (twice the distance root mean square) accuracy

The genuine receiver-autonomous integrity — real HPL/VPL with alert limits and a Stanford diagram — lives in the integrity pack (scenarios/integrity.toml); that’s the Tier-3 reading exercise.

What the tests pin#

  • tutorial3_spoof_analytic_matches_montecarlo — analytic vs MC P_md agree to < 0.05 for both clocks; quantum security_fom > 0.9.
  • The hybrid summary (6600 s / 350 s) and the inertial split are pinned through their teaching scenarios (scenarios/hybrid.toml, scenarios/inertial.toml) by teaching_scenarios_match_their_recorded_expected_summary, which compares each engine summary with the # expected: line the file records. The ½bT² oracle above is the physics check on those numbers.

Pitfalls and units#

  • The timing figure of merit (FoM) is in nanoseconds; the position FoM is in metres and 1-DOF (one degree of freedom: single-axis, not CEP/2DRMS).
  • integrity 0.998 is self-consistency, not aviation integrity.
  • The classical suite’s timing survives only because of the optical time-transfer resync; read the (t …/p …) split, not just the headline PNT-holdover.

Where next#

  • Run the honest-failure case: scenarios/lunanet-araim.toml (kind lunar-integrity) reports a south-pole ARAIM (advanced RAIM) pass where protection levels exceed the alert limit (HPL 263–452 m > 50 m, 0 of 24 epochs available), so the system is reported unavailable: a feature, not a bug.
  • Carry the story into low Earth orbit (LEO): scenarios/leo-resilience-gnss-jammed-leo-carries.toml (kind campaign) chains a GNSS jamming phase, a LEO positioning layer that carries the user through it, and an integrity check on one timeline. See the newer capabilities for the other resilience and vertical scenarios.
  • Defend a result: do the Tier-3 exercise — Monte-Carlo bands, reproducibility, and reading a protection level.
