Partner data: what measured data could promote a MODELLED row

Kshana labels every capability in a machine-checked ledger (verification matrix). Most timing and sensor rows are MODELLED: they rest on stated models, published datasheets and internal checks, and no measured record of the device class has been compared with them. A holder of such a record can change that. This page says which rows, what to send, how a submission is checked, how it is scored and how it is credited. It is written for a laboratory, a manufacturer or a university group; it is not an offer of work and it claims no endorsement by anyone. Advisory only: Kshana is a study instrument, not type-approved navigation or timing equipment.

What the labels mean#

Label Meaning
VALIDATED an independent external oracle agrees with the capability under a bar fixed before the comparison. A measured record from a partner can be that oracle for the sub-claim it covers; the matrix invariants forbid the label without one
MODELLED internally consistent and said out loud; no external oracle covers the claim. A MODELLED row that is compared with measured data and misses stays MODELLED, and the miss is published
PARTNER a discipline the run relies on that a hardware partner owns and Kshana does not provide, for example the figures that describe a partner's own clock or interferometer. Kshana models what such a device would do to a navigation or timing chain; it does not vouch for the device

A submission can move a row to VALIDATED only for the claim the data actually tests, and only if the comparison meets its bar. It cannot make a device's own figures Kshana's to vouch for: those stay the partner's (PARTNER). Whether a row's label changes is the owner's decision, recorded in the commit that changes it, with the oracle text saying exactly what was compared with what.

Rows a partner's data could promote#

Each row below is the exact requirement text of a MODELLED row of the matrix (a test keeps this page and the matrix in step). "Could change" says what a pre-registered comparison with measured data would support if the bar is met; it is never a promise.

Row Data that would test it Could change
GNSS-denied clock holdover a record of days of a clock of a named class, free-running after its last discipline, compared with a better reference, at a sample interval of a minute or less the per-class red-noise floors that bound holdover figures stay MODELLED today; a measured long-averaging-time stability could be compared with the floor of that class, for that class only
Quantum-vs-classical PNT trade & GNSS-denied resilience (13503) the measured Allan deviation, as a table or as the phase record, of a partner's clock or atom interferometer the trade figures quantify (never validate) the partner device, so they would be labelled as the partner's figures (PARTNER); the ingest of measured Allan data into a noise model is already matched to an independent solver
Oscillator holdover presets from public datasheets a phase record of an oscillator of one of the four preset models, over at least a day whether the preset's datasheet-derived noise model reproduces a measured unit's stability at the datasheet's own averaging times
Measured clock device cards (held-out prediction) a phase record of a clock class not yet carded (an OCXO, a rubidium, a caesium, a maser, an optical clock) a card fitted on the first third and predicting the Allan deviation of the rest within the pre-registered factor, as already done for GNSS satellite clocks and one receiver
Clock-log formats linuxptp (ptp4l, phc2sys, ts2phc) and chrony (tracking, statistics) read to a normalised phase series a log from a real installation (any daemon version) with the daemon's own summary lines the readers were built from public line layouts and fixtures; a real log compared with an independent reader would be the first external check
Clock-log formats (RINEX clock, TimeLab ASCII, TAPR TICC) read to a normalised phase series a real counter or analysis-software export the same, for those formats
Holdover test scored from an operator-supplied time-error record a real holdover test: the record, the times the operator removed and restored the reference, and the laboratory's own MTIE, TDEV and mask verdicts from its analysis software an independent computation of the same quantities to compare with the engine's
IMU log ingest, gyro heading input and Allan fit of hybrid sensor parameters a stationary log from an inertial unit whose noise parameters are published or measured by someone else the fit recovered within a stated factor of the independent parameters
Quantum inertial dead-reckoning resilience a static log or the noise parameters of a partner's cold-atom inertial sensor the device figures are the partner's (PARTNER); the position-error budget that composes them stays Kshana's model

Start with the first two: they decide whether the per-class holdover floors and the quantum-versus-classical trade rest on any measured data of their own.

What to send#

A submission is two files in one folder: the data and a small description of it, submission.toml (kshana.partner-submission/1; a worked, made-up example is examples/partner-submission/).

Clock data. One of the formats Kshana reads (see clock-log formats): TAPR TICC logs, TimeLab-style ASCII phase or frequency, RINEX clock, linuxptp ptp4l, phc2sys and ts2phc logs, chrony tracking and statistics logs. Phase is preferred over frequency; state the units and the sample interval. Give the longest uninterrupted record you have: stability at long averaging times needs days, and a record under a day is accepted but warned about.

Inertial data. A named-column CSV (t_s, gx, gy, gz, ax, ay, az, rad/s and m/s^2) or the EuRoC layout, from a stationary device, long enough for the fit (at least 2000 samples; hours is better).

The description says, and the checker verifies where it can:

  • the type of organisation (a type such as "a national timing laboratory", not a name; a name is optional and only for credit);
  • the device (kind, class, and a model for class other);
  • how the data was taken: what it was compared against, the start time (UTC), the environment, and whether it is raw or processed (and what was done to it);
  • the SHA-256 of the data file, so the file scored is the file described;
  • the MODELLED rows it is offered against, by the exact requirement text above;
  • the licence (an SPDX identifier or words) and how the data may be used in the repository: public (committed, with attribution), hash-only (only its hash and the scores are published) or private (the score is shared with the submitter alone);
  • three statements: it is a measurement and not a simulation, the submitter may license it for this use, and the files hold no personal data.

Not to send. Anything personal or with contact details (the checker refuses an email address or telephone number in the description), anything under export control or restricted by agreement, and any simulated data presented as measured.

How a submission is checked#

  1. Format and metadata, by you or us, before anything runs.

    kshana partner check submission.toml          # add --json for a machine-readable report

    It confirms the schema, the required fields, the hash, the licence and attribution rules, that the target rows exist and are MODELLED, that the data reads with the engine's own reader, and it reports what it read (samples, interval, span, gaps) and warns about records that are short. Exit status 0 means complete and readable; 1 lists what to fix. It does not score the data, validate a row or promise it will be used.

  2. A pre-registration, before any scored run. For each row, a document in xval/<name>/ fixes, and is committed before the data is run: the row, the data file's SHA-256, which part of the data is held out (nothing fitted may see it), the metric, and the bar. For clock cards the existing template is the held-out prediction used for the IGS and receiver records: fit on the first third of the record, predict the Allan deviation of the rest, and require the predicted over measured ratio within a stated factor at every fitted averaging time. A bar is never loosened after a run.

  3. One scored run. The result, every number and every miss included, is published with the command and the commit, and a test pins it. A miss is a finding, not a failure to hide: the row stays MODELLED and the page says so.

  4. The ledger decision is the owner's, in writing, in the commit that changes the row. The oracle text of a promoted row names the dataset, its licence and credit, what was compared and what was not.

Credit and rights#

  • The data stays yours under the licence you state. hash-only and private keep the file out of the repository.
  • A public submission is credited by the attribution line you give, in the row's oracle text and in the result file.
  • Nothing is published under your name without your written consent; an organisation named as a source is cited as a source, never as an endorser. No claim of approval or endorsement is made by or about a partner.
  • You can withdraw a submission before its result is published. After that the result stays as recorded; if you withdraw the data licence, the file is removed from the repository and the hash and the scores remain.
  • Kshana transmits nothing and drives no hardware. A submission is files in, a report out.

Outreach#

A one-page note for a laboratory is drafted for the owner in docs/drafts/PARTNER-OUTREACH.md. It is a draft: nothing is sent, and nobody is contacted, until the owner decides.
