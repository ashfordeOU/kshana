Lunar reference station

MODELLED. Under the stated assumptions (the constellation, its signal-in-space error, the station's survey accuracy, the correction latency and the link quantization, each an input labelled with its public source or as ILLUSTRATIVE), the model gives the figures below. They are not a statement about the performance of any real system. Kshana is not affiliated with or endorsed by any agency, and makes no TRL or heritage claim. Advisory only.

Purpose#

Public material describes a lunar south-pole reference station that monitors a lunar navigation service, computes local differential corrections, keeps a lunar time reference on an atomic clock, and carries a laser retroreflector and a VLBI transmitter for the lunar reference frame (the class publicly described for ESA's NovaMoon concept; sources below). Kshana already models each piece separately. This study composes them into one reproducible, open analysis of what such a station could do under public assumptions, and pre-registers it so it can be scored against published results and, eventually, flight results.

Run it#

kshana scenarios/lunar-station-coverage.toml     # about 6 s in a release build

or run_toml from Python, WebAssembly or the MCP server's run_scenario with kind = "lunar-station-coverage".

Coverage and accuracy map (lunar-station-coverage)#

For a differential reference station at a known south-polar site: at each user point on a ring grid from 80° S to the pole and over the region, across a day of epochs and Monte Carlo trials, the percentile corrected and uncorrected position error (3-D, horizontal, vertical), availability with a reason for every unavailable epoch, coverage against a threshold stated before the run, DO-229E protection levels, the coverage radius per azimuth sector, and the correction-link budget at the station and at the worst point. No new physics: it sweeps the tested functions of lunar-differential-pnt and reduces to that kind exactly at one point, one epoch and one trial.

Outputs#

Every numeric field carries a unit and a provenance class in the result's units block (checked by tests/field_units_global.rs). The region block: availability, coverage_fraction, corrected_p_3d_m, corrected_p_h_m, corrected_p_v_m, uncorrected_p_3d_m, improvement_factor, hpl_max_m, vpl_max_m, hpl_below_alert_fraction, worst_point; coverage_radius_km per 30° azimuth sector; points[] per point; and budget_at_station_and_at_edge.

What the checks can and cannot tell#

  • tests/lunar_station_coverage_reference.rs: the reduction to lunar-differential-pnt, the zero-baseline and survey floors, monotonicity, unavailable reasons, determinism, the work guard. These show the sweep is the model it claims to sweep.
  • tests/lunar_station_numpy_oracle.rs: an independent numpy re-computation of the arithmetic on exported cases, pre-registered before it ran. Positions and percentiles met their bars; the station corrections missed theirs by double-precision rounding (recorded, with its numbers, in tests/fixtures/lunar_station/PREREGISTRATION.md).
  • Neither checks the model. The error model, the first-order decorrelation, the survey, ageing and quantization models and every threshold are this project's own or ILLUSTRATIVE.

Terrain horizon masks#

MODELLED. An optional [terrain] section (src/lunar_terrain.rs) gives the station and every user point its own azimuth-dependent horizon, in the 0.36 skyline table format (rows [azimuth_deg, elevation_deg], linear in azimuth with wrap at 360°). A satellite counts at a site when it clears both mask_deg and the horizon at its azimuth; an epoch the horizon leaves with fewer than four satellites at a point, where the flat mask gives four, is reported as unavailable.terrain_masked (user_lt4 keeps its flat meaning). Two sources, combined as their pointwise maximum when both are given:

  • a stated profile, station_horizon or user_horizon (applied at every user point), each with a required source label;
  • a horizon ray-marched over a polar DEM (dem_path): great circles of the lunar sphere with exact curvature, from a stated mast height (default 2 m), every azimuth_step_deg (default 1°), out to max_range_km (default 150 km) or the grid edge. The repository vendors a crop of the public-domain LRO LOLA polar product LDEM_75S_240M, 5 × 5 block-mean resampled to 1200 m and about ±420 km from the pole (data/lunar-dem/NOTICE.md: source URL, SHA-256, licence, crop and resample steps);
  • or, in place of dem_path, a latitude/longitude DEM tile (dem_tile_path) in the realdata::lola_dem text format (a header lat_min lat_max lon_min lon_max n_lat n_lon, then the heights in metres above the mean sphere, row-major), ray-marched the same way, with the latitude spacing setting the default step. The report echoes the tile's path, SHA-256 and extent (terrain.dem_tile). A tile resampled from the vendored crop gives the station the crop's own horizon to 0.22° (tests/lunar_station_terrain.rs). No tile is vendored.

The same masks apply to the moonlight-service-volume grid (terrain, user_horizon and dem_path; a grid point outside the DEM keeps the flat mask and is counted). Without the section every document is byte-identical to a run without it.

Checks: the ray march reproduces closed-form horizons (the bare-sphere dip, a crater in a plateau from its centre and off it, a rasterised cone against the analytic cone); the vendored crop is oriented as the source (Shackleton and Haworth floors low, the Malapert massif high); an independent numpy ray-cast on the crop, pre-registered (tests/fixtures/lunar_terrain/PREREGISTRATION.md), agrees to 5.5e-10 deg at five sites, which checks the arithmetic, not the terrain. No published polar horizon or illumination figure has been reproduced, so the row stays MODELLED (ledger row "Lunar terrain horizon masks from a stated profile or a polar DEM").

scenarios/lunar-station-coverage-terrain.toml runs the bundled inputs on a 2° grid with 100 trials and the DEM horizon at every site. The station site (89° S, 0° E) has a highest horizon of about 4.0°, below the 5° mask, so at the station the terrain does not bind; at the user points the area-weighted mean of each point's highest horizon is 9.7° and the largest 26.3°. Against the same inputs with the flat mask, the region's corrected p95 3-D error rises from 16.3 m to 18.6 m and the largest HPL from 480 m to 62 km. Region availability rises from 91.9 % to 93.0 %: an epoch is unavailable when a point uses a satellite the station does not see, and a horizon that hides that satellite from the point can make the epoch available.

Limitations#

  1. Terrain and horizon masking: addressed in 0.36, with limits. Per-site horizon masks from a stated profile or the vendored LOLA DEM crop are modelled (above). What remains: the vendored crop is 1200 m per pixel, so relief below about a kilometre is smoothed away and block means lower sharp peaks; a ray that leaves the crop (about 420 km from the pole) ends there; there is no diffraction, terrain multipath or partial obstruction; site positions for ranging stay on the mean sphere at the stated heights (the DEM sets the horizon only); the horizon is not validated against an external figure. The per-satellite export site of moonlight-service-volume is masked like a grid point: with a terrain section each export row carries clears_terrain and the report the site's horizon (export_site_terrain); the antenna in-beam tallies still count every link that clears the flat mask.
  2. Multipath and receiver hardware: addressed in 0.36 as an opt-in, with limits. A [receiver] section (src/lunar_receiver.rs) adds elevation-dependent multipath a + b exp(-el / el0) at the user and the station (the DO-229 functional form; on the Moon every value is ILLUSTRATIVE), the station's multipath optionally first-order Gauss-Markov over epochs, early-minus-late DLL code noise from C/N0, and per-channel inter-channel biases. What remains: antenna phase-centre and group-delay variation are not modelled; no lunar measurement fixes the multipath values; the correction-link budget block does not include the receiver terms.
  3. Ephemeris-error growth over the latency: addressed in 0.36 as an opt-in, with limits. orbit_err_growth_m_per_s adds a linear growth of the broadcast orbit error with age (range 1-sigma rate x latency / sqrt(3)), on top of the frozen-error re-projection; linear growth is the first-order term of a propagated state error (checked to 1 % at 600 s against the in-tree two-body propagation). What remains: no public source gives a lunar growth rate (ILLUSTRATIVE); no along-track/radial structure; the correction-link budget block uses the frozen model. The K5 addendum (tests/fixtures/lunar_station_published/ADDENDUM-LRS03.md, pre-registered alone) runs K5 at 0.001, 0.01 and 0.1 m/s: it fails in every case (369.8 m, 2315 m, 22797 m against a 1.5 m bar), as stated before the run.
  4. Solution style (filter, height constraint, three satellites): an opt-in in 0.36, with limits. A [solution] section (src/lunar_solution.rs) adds a height pseudo-measurement, a three-satellite minimum with it, and a static-user weighted least-squares window over epochs (a shared position, a clock per epoch), which stands in for a filter. What remains: it treats the epochs' errors as independent, so it averages down faster than errors correlated in time would; it is static-user only; there is no dynamic filter, carrier phase or ambiguity resolution; the case trace (trace_case) shows the snapshot arithmetic. The published-claims addendum with this mode is tests/fixtures/lunar_station_published/ADDENDUM-LRS06.md (pre-registered alone). With a 1 m height constraint, three satellites and a 6-epoch window (ILLUSTRATIVE settings), the R300 corrected p95 3-D error falls from 280.6 m to 2.11 m (horizontal 0.90 m, not scored); K1 to K5 still fail and K6 passes, 1 of 6 as in the recorded run. The window's independence assumption favours it.

Comparison against published claims#

MODELLED. A comparison of this model, run under the public assumptions, with the claims the sources make. It is not a statement about the performance of any real system.

  • Sources S1 to S5 (title, URL, retrieval date, SHA-256, licence): tests/fixtures/lunar_station/SOURCES.md.
  • Every numeric claim, quoted with its page and stated assumptions and classified as comparable, partly comparable or not comparable: tests/fixtures/lunar_station_published/CLAIMS.md. No claim is fully comparable, because no source states a percentile or the satellites' orbit elements.
  • Bars, cases and inputs, committed alone before the run (revision 2, 05bf9a19), and the recorded run: tests/fixtures/lunar_station_published/PREREGISTRATION.md. The finding is pinned in tests/lunar_station_published_oracle.rs.
bar claim (sources) this model bar verdict
K1 one station gives sub-metre accuracy over the whole south pole, 300 km circle (S1, S2, S3) coverage 0.00 >= 0.95 fail
K2 the same, 600 km circle (S2 p10, S3 p23) coverage 0.00 >= 0.95 fail
K3 sub-metre with code corrections (S1, S2) corrected p95 3-D 280.6 m <= 1.5 m fail
K4 "decimeter level" from pseudorange corrections (S3 p22, S5 p5) 280.6 m <= 0.15 m fail (could not pass at the sources' 0.30 m code noise)
K5 sub-metre kept at 600 s latency (S2 p16) 293.0 m <= 1.5 m fail
K6 the same accuracy from 10 km to 500 km baseline (S2 p17) ratio 1.39 <= 1.5 pass

1 of 6 bars passes. The cause is geometry. No source publishes the constellation's orbit elements. On the ILLUSTRATIVE in-tree elements, a user and the station share four satellites above 5 deg in only 8 % (300 km) and 6 % (600 km) of epochs, at near-singular geometry: the uncorrected p95 is 3.8 km, where the sources quote < 10 m. The sources' own simulations used a filter, a height constraint and three satellites in common view, which this snapshot model does not have. The run therefore does not reproduce the claims on those elements, and it is not evidence that the claims are wrong. The K6 pass is a ratio of two geometry-dominated figures and says nothing about decorrelation. A published set of the constellation's elements would be scored under the same bars as a second, dated run.

Pre-registered prediction#

A pre-registered prediction, frozen at commit c05dac1d on the public-sources inputs, with its scoring protocol: docs/predictions/lunar-reference-station.md. It is a prediction, not a validation, and every figure in it is dominated by the unpublished element set of the four-satellite constellation (the elements used are ILLUSTRATIVE): over the 80 deg S region, availability 8.2 %, corrected p95 3-D 280 m, uncorrected 3.8 km. MODELLED; tests/prediction_frozen.rs fails if any frozen byte moves.

Station time laboratory#

Whether the station's miniRAFS, steered to LTC and then free-running or re-steered, stays within 1, 10 and 100 ns: the optional [time_lab] block of lunar-time-budget (scenarios/lunar-station-time-lab.toml). The miniRAFS reaches 1 ns after 2.8 s, 10 ns after 377 s and 100 ns after 10.5 h; the source's nanosecond-level target is quoted, not adopted. MODELLED. Model, outputs, the time-to-threshold table for four clock classes and the comparison with the sources: docs/lunar-station/time-lab.md.

Station geodesy#

How much one retroreflector and one VLBI beacon at the station site tighten the lunar frame datum beyond the five archived retroreflector arrays: the optional extra_reflectors and extra_beacons inputs of lunar-llr-datum (scenarios/lunar-station-geodesy-baseline.toml and -with-station.toml). Cramer-Rao bounds for a reduced parameter set on an ILLUSTRATIVE schedule and site: the translation norm 1-sigma falls from 0.0185 m to 0.0102 m (ratio 0.551), the scale from 0.00178 to 0.00063 ppm. MODELLED. Method, caveats (a south-polar site sees the Earth low or not at all) and the full Helmert table: docs/lunar-station/geodesy.md.
