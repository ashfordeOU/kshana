Maritime trust

Can the bridge trust this fix?

Open a kshana receiver-trust run for a vessel. Kshana scores every position fix from 0 to 100 from what the receiver already puts on the bus, and says which checks reduced it. Files you open here are read in this page and never uploaded.

Advisory software. Kshana is not type-approved navigation equipment (IEC 61108, IEC 61162) and has not been assessed against any performance standard. The score and the gate are aids to the navigator's judgement. The operator stays responsible for the safe navigation of the vessel and for any decision to rely on, or stop relying on, a fix. A fix with no alarm is not thereby a good fix.

      What this view does and does not show

      • The demo is synthetic text: a made-up NMEA log of a ferry on an illustrative Tallinn to Helsinki route, with a made-up position drag-off partway through. It models no radio signal and is not a measurement or a chart.
      • In the demo the receiver reports a valid fix throughout (GGA quality 1, RMC status A). The trust score is what falls. The gap between the reported and the true track is the point of the view.
      • NMEA-only checks cannot see a spoofer that reproduces the whole constellation with a self-consistent position, velocity, heading, power and clock, nor a replay of recorded real signals. A fix with no alarm is not thereby a good fix.
      • Nothing on this page is a measure of how the checks perform on real interference. The score mapping and every weight are fixed before a log is scored, with no learning and nothing fitted to events.
