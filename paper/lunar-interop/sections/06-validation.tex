\section{Validation and the structure/magnitude firewall}
\label{sec:validation}

A paper that converts a metre-level lunar disagreement into a procurement design law
must be scrupulous about which of its numbers are earned on real data and which are
representative. We enforce this with an explicit firewall, implemented as a rule in
the engine's verification suite and stated plainly here: a quantity is \Validated{}
only when it is checked against an \emph{independent} oracle---a separate dataset,
library, or published vector---to a stated tolerance; a result is \Proven{} when it is
an algebraic theorem; everything else is \Modelled{}. This section states what falls
on each side and gives the external check that anchors the \Validated{} side.

\subsection{The independent oracle for the decomposition}
\label{sec:oracle}

The one load-bearing empirical object of this paper is the rotation-only
decomposition of the real inter-ephemeris disagreement (Table~\ref{tab:anchor}). We
validate it against an independent SciPy/NumPy reference. A generator script
(Section~\ref{sec:availability}) reads the DE440/INPOP21a/EPM2021 kernels through
\code{calceph}, samples the geocentric Moon and the planetary SSB positions on the
same grid used by the engine, and computes the rotation-only Helmert-difference fit
by an independent singular value decomposition of the centred cross-covariance
\citep{arun1987leastsquares}, emitting the expected raw disagreement, rotation
residual, reducible and irreducible metres, and the three rotation magnitudes to a
vendored fixture (\code{reference.json}). Crucially, both the engine and the oracle
consume positions rounded to six decimals, so the comparison is byte-consistent and
tests the \emph{algorithm}, not floating-point sampling noise
(Appendix~\ref{app:methods}). The engine's \code{provenance\_split} reproduces every
value to a relative error below $10^{-3}$ and an absolute error below $\SI{e-3}{m}$
(metre quantities) and $\SI{e-2}{\nano\radian}$ (angle quantities)---including the
near-cancelling INPOP21a--EPM2021 excess, where $\rot_{\mathrm{moon}}$ and
$\rot_{\mathrm{tie}}$ nearly coincide and the subtraction is the numerically hardest.
This is the paper's single \Validated{} row: the decomposition of the \emph{real}
ephemeris disagreement is externally confirmed by an independent implementation.

\subsection{What is Validated, Proven, and Modelled}
\label{sec:matrix}

Table~\ref{tab:honesty} draws the line explicitly. Only the rotation-only
inter-ephemeris decomposition is \Validated{}. The gauge lemma, the removability
structure of the reducible/irreducible split, the triangle bound, and the $\tau(B)$
inversion are \Proven{} (algebra); the affine numeric attribution of the rotation into
a frame-tie and a Moon-excess is a modelling interpretation
(Remark~\ref{rem:attribution}). Everything at the system level---the multi-provider user model,
the budget totals of Table~\ref{tab:budget}, the design-law magnitudes, and the
$\tau(B)$ map of Table~\ref{tab:tolerance}---is \Modelled{}, because the ``providers''
are ephemerides, a representative analog. Two internal checks are honest but not
independent and are labelled so: the full seven-parameter Helmert fit is exercised
only by an analytic-recovery unit test (a synthetic datum is injected and recovered),
and the budget assembly is an internal RMS combination of the validated pairwise
numbers.

\subsection{The split caveat, restated}
\label{sec:splitcaveat}

The firewall has one boundary that is a modelling interpretation rather than a
tolerance, and we restate it here so it is impossible to miss. Attributing the
planet-common rotation to a removable ICRF frame-tie (the \emph{reducible} part) and
the Moon-excess to irreducible dynamics is a stated modelling interpretation
(Remark~\ref{rem:attribution}). It is the reasonable reading---a rotation shared by
the Moon and the planets is naturally a frame-tie---but it is not forced by the data.
The attribution-independent, convention-free claim, the one a skeptical reviewer can
accept without granting the interpretation, is the Moon-\emph{excess} rotation
$|\rot_{\mathrm{exc}}|$: it is by construction the part of the lunar disagreement that
does not appear in the planetary tie, hence unambiguously Moon-specific and removable
by no whole-frame choice. Every irreducible-floor and design-law statement in the
paper rests only on the excess, not on the frame-tie label.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:reproducibility}

Every quantitative result in this paper---the three-pair decomposition of
Table~\ref{tab:anchor}, the budget of Table~\ref{tab:budget}, and the tolerance map
of Table~\ref{tab:tolerance}---is regenerated from the committed engine and the
vendored fixtures by a single command (Section~\ref{sec:availability}). The
\Validated{} row is asserted in the continuous-integration test suite, which enforces
the external-oracle gate: the pairwise decomposition must match \code{reference.json}
to the stated tolerance or the build fails. There are no hand-entered results in the
tables: the manuscript numbers and the engine outputs are the same numbers, traceable
to one run.
