A multi-provider lunar positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) architecture---the
declared vision of LunaNet, ESA Moonlight/LCNS, and their national counterparts---%
requires a number that no interoperability standard yet supplies: the
\emph{consistency tolerance}, the maximum cross-provider frame-and-dynamics
disagreement a user can absorb while staying within a positioning budget. The
LunaNet Interoperability Specification standardizes formats, frames, and time tags,
but its cross-provider consistency tolerance is deferred to a still-unreleased
applicable document---the Lunar Reference System and LunaNet Reference Time System
Standard (AD-5, {LNIS-TBD-AD0005}). We supply it in three parts. First, we prove a
\emph{consistency-tolerance theorem}: to first order the pairwise inter-provider
coordinate difference decomposes into a constant seven-parameter Helmert datum and a
dynamics residual (uniquely where the sampled geometry gives the datum full column
rank). A datum \emph{common} to all providers is invisible to any single-provider
user and cancels in provider-versus-provider fusion; it becomes real position error
only against an absolute external tie. The object that a fusing or hand-off user
actually pays---the ``interop tax''---is instead the \emph{differential} datum that
two providers do not share. That tax splits, as a measured affine decomposition of
the recovered rotation $\rot_{\mathrm{moon}}=\rot_{\mathrm{tie}}+\rot_{\mathrm{exc}}$,
into a \emph{reducible} part a common frame convention removes and an
\emph{irreducible} part that no coordinate convention removes; and inverting a
triangle bound yields the closed-form tolerance $\tau(B)$ per Helmert parameter for a
user budget $B$. Second, we anchor the budget on the only real ``multiple providers''
that exist today---the three independent authoritative lunar ephemerides DE440 (JPL),
INPOP21a (IMCCE), and EPM2021 (IAA~RAS)---and show, on their real 2024--2025
geocentric-Moon disagreement, that the cross-provider inconsistency is an
\emph{orientation} effect: a constant frame rotation explains $71$--$94\%$ of the
disagreement by amplitude ($91$--$99.7\%$ by variance), splitting into a reducible
frame-tie ($\approx\!1$--$3$~nrad) and an irreducible Moon-specific excess rotation
($\approx\!5$--$6$~nrad for the JPL-versus-others pairs). Reported in the
lever-arm-independent rotations, that irreducible excess is a
\emph{geocentric-Moon-state} inconsistency: at the Earth--Moon lever arm it is a
$\approx\!2$~m envelope relevant to ephemeris hand-off and cislunar users, while the
same excess rotation seen by a lunar-\emph{surface} user is only $\approx\!8$~mm. This
decomposition is \Validated{}: reproduced against an independent SciPy singular value
decomposition to a relative and absolute tolerance below $10^{-3}$. Third, we fold the
two into a multi-provider interop budget and a design law: because the irreducible
rotation is about twice the reducible (irreducible fraction
$\|\rot_{\mathrm{exc}}\|/(\|\rot_{\mathrm{tie}}\|+\|\rot_{\mathrm{exc}}\|)=0.687$), a
common format-and-frame tag---what today's standards mandate---leaves the dominant
floor (a $\approx\!1.8$~m geocentric-Moon-state envelope at the Earth--Moon lever
arm); closing it requires mandating a \emph{common (or consistency-bounded) dynamical
reference}, not merely a common format. The scope AD-5 itself declares---a lunar
gravity field to finite degree and order plus a parametric precession/nutation
model---is exactly the lunar-orbit dynamics we identify as the irreducible floor. The
theorem, gauge lemma, and tolerance inversion are proven; the inter-ephemeris
decomposition is Validated on real data; the system-level budget and $\tau(B)$ map are
explicitly \Modelled{} (the ``providers'' are ephemerides, a representative
analog---no two lunar-PNT providers fly), and every number is reproducible from a
single command.
