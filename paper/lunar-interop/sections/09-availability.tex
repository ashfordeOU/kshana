\label{sec:availability}
The cross-provider interoperability substrate is part of the open-source
\code{kshana} engine \citep{baweja2026kshana}
(\url{https://github.com/ashfordeOU/kshana}; AGPL-3.0-only). The Helmert-difference
fit, the rotation-only provenance decomposition, the reducible/irreducible split, the
multi-provider budget, and the consistency-tolerance inversion are in the module
\code{lunar\_interop\_budget} (functions \code{provenance\_split},
\code{interop\_budget}, and \code{consistency\_tolerance}). Every number in this
paper is regenerated by
\begin{quote}
  \code{cargo run -{}-example p2\_cross\_provider\_interop}
\end{quote}
which prints Part~A (the per-pair rotation-only decomposition of
Table~\ref{tab:anchor}), Part~B (the multi-provider budget of
Table~\ref{tab:budget}), and Part~C (the consistency-tolerance map of
Table~\ref{tab:tolerance}), directly from the committed engine and the vendored
fixtures. The independent SciPy/NumPy oracle is
\code{scripts/gen\_interop\_ref.py}, which reads the DE440/INPOP21a/EPM2021 kernels
through IMCCE \code{calceph} and emits the reference decomposition; its output, the
sampled geocentric-Moon and planetary-SSB positions, and the provenance notice are
vendored at \code{tests/fixtures/inter\_ephemeris/} (\code{moon\_geo.csv},
\code{planet\_ssb.csv}, \code{reference.json}, \code{NOTICE.md}). The ephemeris
kernels themselves are not vendored (they are large and their redistribution terms
vary); the notice records their public download URLs. The \Validated{} row---the
engine's decomposition matching the SciPy oracle to the stated tolerance---is
asserted in the continuous-integration suite by
\code{tests/lunar\_interop\_budget\_reference.rs}, which fails the build on any
mismatch. All frame, timescale, and sampling conventions are recorded in
Appendix~\ref{app:methods} and the vendored notice.
