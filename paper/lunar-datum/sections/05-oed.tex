\section{Cost-aware optimal experiment design}
\label{sec:oed}

The radial-diversity law of Section~\ref{sec:radial} tells a realization programme
\emph{what} to buy. Real programmes also face \emph{how much} to buy: each
technique carries a cost, and a finite budget must be allocated across a menu of
candidate campaigns. We cast this as gauge-constrained optimal experiment design
(OED) and compute the trade explicitly.

\subsection{The design problem}
\label{sec:designproblem}

Let a menu of candidate measurement campaigns be indexed $b=1,\dots,B$, each with
a precomputed datum Fisher contribution $\Fisher_b$ \eqref{eq:fisher} (assembled
over its own schedule and gates, and preconditioned identically) and a relative
cost $c_b>0$. A design is a subset $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{1,\dots,B\}$; by
additivity its information is $\Fisher_{\mathcal{A}}=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}}\Fisher_b$
and its degeneracy metric $\mu(\mathcal{A})=\lmin(\Schur(\Fisher_{\mathcal{A}}))$.
The budget-constrained design problem is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}^\star(C) \;=\; \arg\max_{\mathcal{A}}\;
  \mu(\mathcal{A})
  \quad\text{subject to}\quad
  \sum_{b\in\mathcal{A}} c_b \le C.
  \label{eq:oed}
\end{equation}
We choose the objective $\mu=\lmin(\Schur)$ deliberately over the classical
scalar OED criteria (D-, A-, E-optimality on the full $7\times7$ matrix): the
programme's binding constraint is the \emph{specific} origin--scale degeneracy, and
$\mu$ measures exactly that marginal, gauge-fixed subproblem
\citep{pukelsheim2006oed}.

\subsection{A greedy heuristic, verified against an exact oracle}
\label{sec:greedy}

Problem \eqref{eq:oed} is a combinatorial subset selection; the exact optimum is
found by enumerating all $2^B$ subsets, which we retain as a brute-force oracle for
menus of modest size. For scalability we also implement a forward-greedy heuristic:
at each step add the affordable campaign maximizing the marginal gain per unit
cost, $\bigl(\mu(\mathcal{A}\cup\{b\})-\mu(\mathcal{A})\bigr)/c_b$, stopping when no
affordable campaign yields a positive gain. Greedy is a heuristic---it can be
sub-optimal on adversarial menus, and we do not claim the objective is
submodular---so we \emph{verify it against the exact oracle} rather than assume
optimality: on a constructed representative menu the greedy design equals the
exhaustive optimum at every budget, and the invariant
$\mu(\text{exhaustive})\ge\mu(\text{greedy})$ is asserted in the test suite. The
greedy heuristic is a \Modelled{} decision tool whose \emph{agreement} with the
oracle is verified; where the two could diverge, the oracle governs, and any menu
on which greedy is knowingly sub-optimal is documented rather than papered over.
The near-optimality of greedy for monotone diminishing-returns objectives is
classical \citep{nemhauser1978submodular}; our verification is empirical and
menu-specific.

\subsection{The cost/degeneracy Pareto frontier}
\label{sec:frontier}

Sweeping the budget $C$ through \eqref{eq:oed} traces the cost/degeneracy Pareto
frontier: the best degeneracy-breaking a programme can achieve at each spend. We
evaluate it over a representative four-campaign menu---LLR (cost $1$), a VLBI limb
baseline (cost $3$), a near-side orbiter (cost $4$), and a far-side orbiter
(cost $5$)---whose beacons, schedules, per-technique noise, and relative costs are
representative \Modelled{} choices, itemized in Appendix~\ref{app:methods} and the
reproducibility ledger. Table~\ref{tab:frontier} and Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}
give the result.

Two features of the frontier carry the design message. First, its shape:
the largest single step is the far-side orbiter, consistent with
Table~\ref{tab:multitech}. Second, and more telling, the optimizer's \emph{order}
of purchase. At a budget of $8$---enough to afford LLR plus either the VLBI
baseline (total cost $4$) or the far-side orbiter (total cost $6$)---the optimizer
chooses the more expensive far-side orbiter and leaves budget unspent, because its
gain-per-cost ($9.33\times10^3/5 \approx 1.9\times10^3$) dominates the VLBI
baseline's ($1.16\times10^3/3 \approx 3.9\times10^2$). The far-side orbiter breaks
the degeneracy $\approx\!4.8\times$ more efficiently \emph{per unit cost} than the
VLBI baseline (and $8.0\times$ in raw gain). Under a realization budget, depth
diversity is not merely effective---it is the rational first purchase.

\begin{remark}[What the frontier is and is not]
The frontier is a \emph{parameterized trade curve} whose axes are a relative cost
and a relative, preconditioned degeneracy metric. Its \emph{shape and ordering}
are the deliverable: LLR alone is degenerate, radial diversity is the efficient way
off that floor, and transverse VLBI is a secondary refinement. The absolute
numbers are \Modelled{} and are not a recommended budget for any specific mission;
substituting a programme's true costs and noise floors into the same machinery
yields its own frontier.
\end{remark}
